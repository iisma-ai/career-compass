# 01 - Interest theory (Holland/RIASEC) and outcomes

Researched Nov 2026. Evidence levels per `.claude/skills/evidence-and-citations/SKILL.md` (A meta-analysis/official docs, B large study, C small/thesis/conf., D vendor/press/blog, E own experiments).
Method note: most publisher PDFs were paywalled or unreadable by the fetch tool, so many numbers come from publisher/university abstract pages or from search-engine summaries of abstracts. Each item says which. Anything not seen on a primary abstract is `[UNVERIFIED]`.

## Question
How valid is Holland's RIASEC model as the backbone of Career Compass? Specifically: (a) hexagon/circumplex structure and Prediger's dimensions, (b) stability of interests across age, (c) do interests/congruence predict performance, satisfaction, persistence, turnover, (d) link to Big Five, (e) work values.

## Key findings

### Structure (hexagon, Prediger dimensions)
- Rounds & Tracey (1996): structural meta-analysis of 20 U.S. ethnic-minority matrices, 76 international matrices from 18 countries, and a 73-matrix U.S. benchmark. Holland's circular-order model did NOT hold across cultures. Gati's 3-group partition and an alternative 3-class partition fit the U.S. benchmark and international samples about equally well; none of the three models fit the U.S. ethnic samples adequately. (A; abstract page via University of Illinois experts) [Rounds & Tracey 1996](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/cross-cultural-structural-equivalence-of-riasec-models-and-measur/)
- Tracey & Rounds (1993, Psychological Bulletin 113, 229-246) is the structural meta-analysis supporting the circular order in U.S. samples. I did not retrieve its abstract or fit numbers; only citing sources describe it (secondary). `[UNVERIFIED: effect sizes]`. Bibliographic record only: [search result](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/an-examination-of-the-structure-of-roes-eight-interest-fields/) is NOT the paper itself; the 1993 claim rests on later citing papers.
- Prediger dimensions (Data-Ideas, People-Things) underlie the hexagon (Prediger 1982; described in secondary sources). **Exact Prediger (1981/1982) weights for Data-Ideas were not found.** What was verified:
  - Things/People score from RIASEC (Prediger-style hexagon weights, secondary source quoting Prediger, Swaney & Mau 1993): `T/P = 2.00R + 1.00I - 1.00A - 2.00S - 1.00E + 1.00C` (types absent from a 3-letter code get 0). Seen in a search summary of a PDF, not read directly. (C/secondary) [search-summary of Prediger et al. 1993](https://typeset.io/pdf/extending-holland-s-hexagon-procedures-counseling-2ypl56psrk.pdf)
  - Strong Interest Inventory Personal Style Scales (using Tracey et al. 2005 formulas, on RIASEC standard scores): `People-Things = 2R + I - A - 2S - E + C`; `Data-Ideas = 1.73E + 1.73C - 1.73I - 1.73A`. (D/vendor documentation; fetched page) [Strong technical supplement, ch. 4](https://strongsupplement.themyersbriggs.com/chapter4.html)
  - Prediger D/I and T/P score ranges for 3-letter codes were stated as about -10..+10 (D/I) and -11..+11 (T/P) in a search summary (secondary). `[UNVERIFIED]`
  - Geometry note (our interpretation): on a regular hexagon with R,I,A,S,E,C at 60-degree steps, T/P loads on the R-S axis and D/I on the I/A vs E/C axis; the 1.73 = sqrt(3) factor reflects cos/sin 30 degrees. Use projection onto the two axes of the 2-D hexagon rather than trusting any single published constant until checked in the original paper.
- Mount, Barrick, Scullen & Rounds (2005, Personnel Psychology 58, 447-478): an 11 x 11 meta-analytic true-score correlation matrix of Big Five + Big Six interests; cluster analysis and nonmetric MDS gave three dimensions: Interests vs Personality traits; Striving for Accomplishment vs Striving for Personal Growth; Interacting with People vs Interacting with Things. (A) [Mount et al. 2005 abstract](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Higher-order-dimensions-of-the-Big-Five/9984963096602771)

### Big Five <-> RIASEC
- Larson, Rottinghaus & Borgen (2002, J Vocational Behavior 61, 217-239): 24 samples linking SDS/Strong/VPI to NEO. Strongest pairs: Artistic-Openness r = .48, Enterprising-Extraversion r = .41, Social-Extraversion r = .31, Investigative-Openness r = .28, Social-Agreeableness r = .19. (A; numbers from an academia.edu abstract listing, via search summary; one-hop) [listing](https://siu.academia.edu/PatrickRottinghaus)
- Barrick, Mount & Gupta (2003, Personnel Psychology 56, 45-74) also meta-analysed FFM x Holland; conclusion: related but not substitutes. Exact table not retrieved `[UNVERIFIED: values]`. [record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/META-ANALYSIS-OF-THE-RELATIONSHIP-BETWEEN-THE/9984963107202771)
- Implication of both: interests overlap only moderately with personality (max r about .5), so interest data are not redundant with Big Five.

### Stability across age
- Low, Yoon, Roberts & Rounds (2005, Psychological Bulletin 131(5), 713-737): quantitative review of longitudinal studies (66 studies per a later citing paper, secondary) from about age 12 to 40. Interest stability unchanged during much of adolescence, increased dramatically in college years (about age 18-21.9) and stayed high for the next two decades; interests in hands-on physical (Realistic) and artistic activities were more stable than science, social, enterprising, clerical interests; longer retest interval lowers stability; profile stability exceeded rank-order stability; interests more stable than personality traits at comparable ages. (A; abstract summary via fetch of a bibliographic listing) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.131.5.713)
- A 2025 paper cites adjusted rank-order stability of .55-.58 for ages 12-18 (secondary). `[UNVERIFIED against Low et al.]` [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1498424)

### Interests and performance, turnover
- Nye, Su, Rounds & Drasgow (2012, Perspectives on Psychological Science 7(4), 384-403): 60 studies, about 568 correlations; interests relate to performance and persistence in work and academic settings; congruence indices correlate more strongly with performance than interest scores alone. (A; abstract via search summary of publisher/Iowa records) [PPS](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691612449021)
- Nye, Su, Rounds & Drasgow (2017, JVB 98, 138-151): 92 studies, 1,858 correlations; congruence-performance baseline r = .32 vs interest-score-performance r = .16. (A; fetched abstract page) [Illinois experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/interest-congruence-and-performance-revisiting-recent-meta-analyt/)
- Van Iddekinge, Roth, Putka & Lanivich (2011, J Applied Psychology 96, 1167-1194): 74 studies, 141 samples (fetched Memphis abstract page; a conference abstract said 77/144, so I use the publisher-linked count). Corrected validities for single interest scales: job performance .14, training performance .26, turnover intentions -.19, actual turnover -.15. Job-specific scales .23 for performance; single RIASEC scales .10; basic-interest scales .11. Not corrected for range restriction. Highest when multiple interests combined. (A) [Memphis](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/11916)

### Interests and satisfaction
- Hoff, Song, Wee, Phan & Rounds (2020, JVB): 105 studies, 39,602 participants, 1949-2016. Interest fit significantly predicts satisfaction but "not as strong as people expect"; stronger link to performance than satisfaction. Coefficient not retrieved `[UNVERIFIED]`. (A) [ScienceDaily summary](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/11/201111144353.htm); [record](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/interest-fit-and-job-satisfaction-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis/10013203533)
- Earlier quantitative review (Earl 2015 thesis, per a search summary): corrected rho = .20 for matched-scale congruence, rho = .08 (95% CI .04-.11) when congruence indices used. (C/secondary) The skill file cites the same range ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/08948453211033903)).
- Kristof-Brown, Zimmerman & Johnson (2005, Personnel Psychology 58, 281-342): P-J and P-O fit meta-analysis (all fit types, not only interest). Second-hand figures: satisfaction r = .56 (P-J), .44 (P-O) `[UNVERIFIED against original]`. Different construct from interest fit; do not conflate. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2005.00672.x)

### Persistence in college major
- Allen & Robbins (2010, J Counseling Psychology 57(1), 23-35): longitudinal data from 15 four-year (n = 3,072) and 13 two-year (n = 788) institutions; interest-major congruence had a direct effect on timely degree completion in both settings; motivation acted indirectly via first-year performance. (B) [record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_816531011) (abstract page via search; effect sizes not seen `[UNVERIFIED]`).
- Tracey & Robbins (2006, JVB 69, 64-89): secondary sources report 80,574 students at 87 colleges over 5 years; congruence predicted 5-year GPA better than ACT scores and related to retention. `[UNVERIFIED: not read]` (B/secondary) [NCDA summary](https://www.ncda.org/aws/NCDA/pt/sd/news_article/24844/_self/layout_ccmsearch/false)
- ACT data byte (3,860 students, 28 institutions): at each ACT Composite level, degree-attainment rates were higher for students with positive interest-major correlation. (D, vendor-affiliated) [ACT](https://act.org/content/dam/act/unsecured/documents/5837-data-byte-2016-9-timely-degree-attainment-rates.pdf)

### Work values
- O*NET Work Values: six values (Achievement, Independence, Recognition, Relationships, Support, Working Conditions) with element IDs 1.B.2.a-... and O*NET notes Work Values data frozen at release 30.2 (skill file). Dictionary pages confirm the element family; the structure of Theory of Work Adjustment / Minnesota Importance Questionnaire (21 needs) was NOT verified `[UNVERIFIED]`. (A for O*NET) [O*NET dictionary example](https://www.onetcenter.org/dictionary/30.0/text/work_values.html)

## Gaps / conflicts
- Congruence predicts performance (r .32) better than satisfaction (modest, rho .08-.20 depending on index). "Interests != satisfaction" must appear in user-facing copy.
- Circumplex fits U.S. majority samples, less so ethnic-minority/international samples (Rounds & Tracey 1996). Spanish/EU validity of the hexagon not checked here (see note 06).
- Exact Prediger D/I weights and Tracey & Rounds 1993 fit indices not retrieved. Weights for T/P and D/I given above are from secondary/vendor sources.
- Interest-performance correlations are modest (.14-.32); interest-based advice is one input, not a prediction of success.
- Most studies are U.S., adult/college samples; adolescent (14-17) evidence is thinner and interests are less stable before about 18 (Low et al.).

## Implications for Career Compass
- Treat RIASEC as a profile with uncertainty, not a 3-letter label: interests stabilise around 18-22, so for teens show "current interest profile" and invite retesting. Show profile shape (all six scores) and not only top-3 code.
- Compute and display Prediger-style 2-D coordinates (People-Things, Data-Ideas) because they are robust cross-culturally (supported by Su et al. note 06); verify weights with E2 factor analysis on OpenPsychometrics data (E, to do).
- Check hexagon fit on our own data (E2) and by gender/language before relying on circumplex-based congruence.
- Prefer congruence on a full profile (e.g. correlation/Euclidean-type) over a single-letter match; Nye et al. show congruence outperforms scale scores for performance.
- Copy: "careers worth exploring" is justified: effects are modest and satisfaction link weak.
- Optionally add a short values or Big-Five-lite module later; Big Five overlaps interests only to r about .5 so it may add incremental information (not tested here).
